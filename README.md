# FIFA World Cup Data Analysis

This project explores historical FIFA World Cup data to examine which countries have been the most successful, whether host nations have an advantage, and how scoring has changed over time.

## Key Findings

Brazil has been the most successful World Cup nation with **5 titles**, followed by Germany and Italy with **4 each**. Hosting also appears to provide a significant advantage: host nations won **68.75%** of their matches compared with **39.03%** for non-host teams. Even after accounting for historical team strength, hosts had about **3.1 times the odds of winning**.

## Host Advantage

A comparison of countries when hosting versus when playing in other World Cups provides further evidence of a host advantage. **Most host countries had a higher match win rate when hosting than when they were not hosting.**

Countries such as Uruguay, England, Sweden, South Korea, France, and Italy showed noticeable improvements when playing on home soil. This comparison is especially useful because it compares countries against **their own historical performance** rather than comparing hosts with completely different teams.

However, the advantage was not universal. Spain performed worse when hosting, while Brazil and the United States showed relatively small differences. Overall, the results suggest that hosting can provide a meaningful advantage, but it does not guarantee success.
