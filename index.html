Accounting for Team Strength

team_strength <- team_matches %>%
  filter(!is_host) %>%
  group_by(team) %>%
  summarise(
    historical_win_rate = mean(won),

team_matches <- team_matches %>%
  left_join(team_strength, by = "team")

Conclusion

Overall, the results suggest that hosting the World Cup is associated with a meaningful competitive advantage. Host nations won 68.75% of their matches compared with 39.03% for non-host teams, and this difference was statistically significant. Even after accounting for historical team strength, host nations still had about 3.1 times the odds of winning. However, the size of the host advantage has varied considerably between tournaments, with no clear trend over time.

These results suggest that team strength alone does not fully explain why hosts have historically performed well. Factors such as home crowd support, familiar playing conditions, reduced travel, and other advantages of playing at home could help explain the difference. However, this analysis shows an association rather than causation, and future analysis could account for factors such as opponent strength and tournament format.
